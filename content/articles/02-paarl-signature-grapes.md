# Paarl's Signature Grapes

Four grapes carry the district. Here is what each one does in Paarl, and what it reminds you of if your reference points are European.

## Chenin Blanc

Once planted here purely for volume, now the Cape's most exciting white. Paarl's old dryland bush vines — many over 35 years old — produce tiny crops of intense fruit.

**Tastes like:** quince, baked apple, honeysuckle, a lanolin waxiness, and acidity that keeps it upright. Wooded versions add almond and toast.

**If you drink:** Vouvray sec or a good white Rioja, this is your bottle.

**Eat with:** roast chicken, pork belly, Cape Malay curry, mature cheddar.

## Shiraz

Paarl's granite slopes give Shiraz a savoury, peppery character rather than the jammy sweetness people expect from warm climates.

**Tastes like:** black pepper, dried herbs, blackberry, cured meat, fine dusty tannin.

**If you drink:** Crozes-Hermitage or a northern Rhône syrah, you are already home.

**Eat with:** lamb on the braai, boerewors, anything smoky.

## Cabernet Sauvignon

Best from the cooler Simonsberg-Paarl slopes. Structured, but with a warmth that Bordeaux rarely shows.

**Tastes like:** blackcurrant, cedar, graphite, tobacco leaf, firm tannin that softens over five to ten years.

**If you drink:** Médoc or Napa Cabernet, this sits between the two.

**Eat with:** dry-aged steak, venison, hard cheese.

## Pinotage

South Africa's own crossing of Pinot Noir and Cinsault, born in 1925 and unfairly maligned for decades. Modern Paarl Pinotage is a different wine to the burnt-rubber caricature.

**Tastes like:** mulberry, plum, smoke, sometimes a hint of coffee and chocolate in the "coffee style" versions.

**If you drink:** Zinfandel or a ripe Douro red, try it.

**Eat with:** barbecue, sticky ribs, bobotie.

## Worth knowing: the supporting cast

Viognier, Grenache, Mourvèdre, Cinsault, Chardonnay and Petit Verdot all appear across the district, mostly in blends and increasingly on their own.
