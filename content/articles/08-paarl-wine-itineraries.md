# Wine Tourism in Paarl: A One-Day and a Weekend Itinerary

Paarl is close enough to Cape Town for a day trip and interesting enough to hold you for a weekend. Here are both versions.

## The one-day route (from Cape Town)

**09:00 — Drive out.** The N1 takes about 55 minutes without traffic. Leave before 08:00 on a weekday or you will sit in it.

**10:00 — Fairview.** Start with wine and cheese on the slopes of Paarl Mountain. Rhône varieties, a goat tower, and an easy introduction to the district.

**11:45 — Vondeling or Perdeberg.** Head to the Paardeberg side for old bush vine Chenin Blanc and a completely different landscape — dry, windy, wide open. Book Vondeling ahead.

**13:30 — Lunch.** Rhebokskloof for the amphitheatre setting, or Backsberg if you would rather braai your own.

**15:30 — Laborie or Landskroon.** Finish close to town so the drive back is short. Laborie is walkable from Paarl's Main Street if you are staying over.

**17:00 — Return.** Three estates, one lunch, no rush.

## The weekend route

### Friday

Arrive late afternoon. Check in around Paarl or Simondium. Dinner in town on Main Street — Paarl has a decent, unshowy restaurant scene that most visitors skip in favour of Franschhoek.

### Saturday

**09:30 — Avondale.** Book the eco wine safari. It is the best explanation of biodynamic farming you will get anywhere in the Cape, and the wines justify the theory.

**12:00 — Babylonstoren.** Garden tour, then a long lunch. Book weeks ahead in season.

**15:30 — Glen Carlou.** Chardonnay and contemporary art on the Simonsberg side, with the valley opening out below.

**Evening.** Dinner at your guesthouse or back in town. You will not want a fourth tasting.

### Sunday

**10:00 — Paarl Mountain Nature Reserve.** Walk off Saturday. The granite domes give you the whole valley at once, and it is free.

**12:00 — Anura or Landskroon.** A relaxed final tasting, cheese, and buying decisions.

**14:00 — Drive home** with a boot full of wine.

## Where to stay

Paarl has guesthouses in town, farm cottages on the estates and a handful of larger hotels. Staying on a farm is worth the small premium — you wake up in the vineyards and you are five minutes from the first tasting.

## Budgeting

Per person, per day, excluding accommodation: roughly R400 to R700 for tastings and lunch, plus a driver split across the group. Wine purchases are extra and always higher than planned.
