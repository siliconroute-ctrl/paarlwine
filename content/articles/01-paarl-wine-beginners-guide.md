# A Beginner's Guide to Paarl Wine

Paarl sits about an hour north-east of Cape Town, wrapped around a giant dome of granite that gave the town its name — *peral*, the pearl, because the rock shines after rain. It is one of the oldest wine districts in South Africa and one of the least understood, largely because it does so many things at once.

## The lay of the land

Paarl is not one place. It is at least four:

- **Paarl Mountain** — decomposed granite soils on the slopes, holding water well into a dry summer. Rhône reds and serious Chenin Blanc.
- **Voor-Paardeberg** — higher, windier, poorer soils to the north-west. Home to old dryland bush vines and some of the most precise white wine in the Cape.
- **Simonsberg-Paarl** — the cooler eastern flank shared with Stellenbosch. Cabernet Sauvignon and Chardonnay with real structure.
- **Klein Drakenstein and the valley floor** — warmer, richer, generous reds built for early drinking.

That variety is why two bottles both marked "Paarl" can taste nothing alike.

## The climate in one paragraph

Hot, dry summers with cold, wet winters — a Mediterranean pattern, closer to the Rhône or inland Spain than to Bordeaux. Big day-to-night temperature swings on the mountain slopes keep acidity in the grapes even when daytime highs are punishing. Most of the best vineyards are either high, exposed to the south-easterly wind, or both.

## What Paarl does best

**Chenin Blanc.** The Cape's signature grape, and Paarl's old dryland bush vines give it a concentration that irrigated young vineyards cannot. Expect quince, yellow apple, a waxy texture and firm acidity.

**Shiraz and Rhône blends.** Warm slopes, granite soils and a generation of winemakers who looked to the Rhône rather than Bordeaux. Peppery, savoury, medium to full bodied.

**Cabernet Sauvignon.** Mainly from the Simonsberg side, where cooler air keeps the tannins fine rather than baked.

**Cap Classique.** Traditional-method sparkling wine, made the same way as Champagne, generally from Chardonnay and Pinot Noir.

## What to buy first

If you are starting from nothing, buy three bottles: an old-vine Chenin Blanc, a Paarl Shiraz and a Simonsberg Cabernet. That trio explains the district faster than any tasting note.
