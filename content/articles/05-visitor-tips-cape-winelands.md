# What Overseas Visitors Should Know Before a Cape Winelands Tasting

The Cape Winelands are unusually welcoming — no appointments-only culture, no snobbery, and prices that will surprise anyone arriving from Europe or North America. A few practical things still catch visitors out.

## Costs

A tasting of five or six wines typically costs between R80 and R250 per person. Many estates waive the fee if you buy a bottle. Wines that would cost €30 in Europe often sell at the cellar door for a third of that.

## Booking

Walk-ins are fine at most large estates. Smaller producers — Vondeling, Avondale, many boutique cellars — require appointments. Between mid-December and mid-January, and over Easter, book everything.

## Getting around

South Africa's drink-driving limit is 0.05% and enforcement is real. Options:

- Hire a driver for the day (roughly R1,200 to R2,500 for a group)
- Use a metered app service between estates — coverage in Paarl is decent but not instant
- Nominate a driver and have them spit, which every tasting room expects and provides for

Public transport between estates does not exist in any practical sense.

## Timing

Most tasting rooms run 10:00 to 16:30, with the last tasting 30 to 45 minutes before closing. Three estates in a day is comfortable. Four is a stretch. Five is a bad idea.

## Etiquette

- Spitting is normal and respectful, not rude.
- Ask questions — pourers are usually knowledgeable and rarely rushed.
- Tipping is not required for tastings but R20 to R50 for a good pourer is appreciated.
- Children are welcome at most estates, and many have gardens or play areas.

## Taking wine home

Check your allowance before you buy. Most countries permit one to two litres duty-free; the EU allows four litres of still wine per adult. Estates and specialist shippers can send cases home for you — expect two to six weeks and a shipping cost that only makes sense above six bottles.

Wine travels fine in checked luggage if wrapped and padded; buy a purpose-made wine sleeve rather than trusting a jumper.

## Seasons

- **Harvest (February–April)** — cellars busy, atmosphere electric, some tours restricted
- **Autumn (April–May)** — vineyards turning gold, quiet, ideal
- **Winter (June–August)** — fires lit, deep discounts, some estates on reduced hours
- **Spring–summer (September–January)** — green, hot, busiest
