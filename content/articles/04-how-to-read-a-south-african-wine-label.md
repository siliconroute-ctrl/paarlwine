# How to Read a South African Wine Label

South African labels are more honest than most, once you know which words are legally defined and which are marketing.

## The WO seal

**WO** stands for Wine of Origin. If a label carries a WO area, the wine is certified: at least 100% of the grapes came from that area, the vintage is at least 85% accurate, and the stated variety is at least 85% of the blend. It is verified by an independent body, not the producer.

WO areas nest inside each other, from broad to narrow:

- **Geographical unit** — e.g. Western Cape
- **Region** — e.g. Coastal Region
- **District** — e.g. Paarl
- **Ward** — e.g. Voor-Paardeberg, Simonsberg-Paarl

The narrower the area, the more specific the claim. "WO Voor-Paardeberg" tells you far more than "WO Western Cape".

## Estate, cellar and single vineyard

- **Estate wine** — grown, made and bottled on a registered unit of land. The strictest term.
- **Wine of Origin [district]** — grapes from that district, not necessarily one farm.
- **Single vineyard** — a registered block of no more than six hectares, one variety. Rare and meaningful.

## Sugar terms on whites

- **Dry** — up to 4 g/l residual sugar
- **Off-dry / semi-dry** — roughly 4 to 12 g/l
- **Semi-sweet** — roughly 12 to 30 g/l
- **Natural sweet** — over 20 g/l, sweetness from the grape

## Sparkling wine

**Method Cap Classique (MCC)** means bottle-fermented, the same method as Champagne. Anything simply labelled "sparkling wine" or "carbonated" is not.

## Words with no legal meaning

*Reserve*, *limited release*, *barrel select*, *private collection*. These are producer language. They may signal a better wine; they guarantee nothing.

## The bar code trick

A bar code beginning 600 or 601 tells you the wine was bottled in South Africa. Useful when a label is deliberately vague.
